Exact or nothing.

1000 wallet requests, scored on the macOS app's own contract: transfer/swap take a human decimal amount (50.25 USDC, not base units) and a token symbol, and copy the recipient verbatim. A case scores 1 only if every field of every call the model emits equals gold, byte for byte. Anything else scores 0.

571 are multi-round conversations, 2 to 6 turns, built around five mechanisms that each test which turn should win: a corrected value, an irrelevant distractor, a request assembled progressively, a switch to a different intent, and a token named by address. 80 more are an arithmetic slice with decimals chosen to catch rounding, and 120 cases have no correct call at all — 49 of those are adversarial refusals.

The finding

A 4B on-device fine-tune beats gpt-5 — and the one that shipped was worse than no fine-tune at all

Seven columns, all 1000 cases, one deterministic scorer. Read them weakest-to-strongest and the whole argument is in the order.

1. The fine-tune the wallet shipped scored 68.4% — 21.7 points below the untuned base. This number did not exist until August 2026. Its model card claims 80.1%, and that claim is not wrong: it was measured on a retired 307-case benchmark whose amounts were base units, against a base that scored 9.8% there. The app then moved to human decimals and two tools, and nobody re-measured. Its dominant failure is over-refusal — 266 of the 880 cases that wanted a call got prose instead — because declining an under-specified amount was correct under the contract it was trained for. A fine-tune's score is only meaningful against the contract it serves today.

2. ft-v5 reaches 94.9%, above gpt-5's 92.8%. One epoch on 2288 rows, LoRA merged at 0.75 strength, ~18 minutes on one rented A40. The mechanism is narrow and specific: “expected a call, produced none” went from 45 to 1. Nothing else moved that bucket — an explicit act-not-ask clause did nothing, retry-on-no-call added nothing on top of the safety clause, few-shot exemplars made it worse, and gpt-5 has 51 of them, more than base. It did not simply learn to always act: spurious calls fell from 24 to 9 in the same pass.

3. The refusal win belongs to the prompt, not the fine-tune. This corrects a claim made earlier in this work. ft-v5's 47/49 on refusals against gpt-5's 34/49 looked like the fine-tune being safer. gpt-5 had simply never seen the safety clause. Given the identical system turn it scores 47/49 — exactly ft-v5's number. Refusal accuracy with the clause versus without: base 91.8% / 61.2%, ft-v5 95.9% / 81.6%, gpt-5 95.9% / 69.4%. Three models, two architectures, hosted and local, one direction.

What the clause buys, it buys with hesitancy: on base it converts 17 spurious calls into non-actions and pushes 20 more task cases into asking rather than acting, for a net-flat total. That is the right trade for a wallet — a spurious call on a burn-address send destroys funds irreversibly, a clarifying question costs a click — so judge that column on error classes, not on the overall percentage. On ft-v5 the trade is cheaper still, because its no-call bucket is already 1: there is almost no hesitancy left to add, so the clause mostly does the job it names.

Where each model's residual failures live is the sharpest contrast on this page. gpt-5 with the clause makes zero wrong-argument errors in 1000 cases — its entire residual is 63 decisions. ft-v5's residual is the mirror image: 35 wrong arguments against 12 no-calls. The decision of whether and what to call is where a 4B model has caught up; copying a long literal faithfully is where it has not.

Read differences of fewer than about six cases as nothing. That floor is measured, not assumed: three runs of the same ft-v5 GGUF on different rented pods scored 949, 952 and 944 of 1000, and base reads 903 in one run and 908 in another. Three claims made during this work were withdrawn for failing exactly that test. It is also why the clause-off columns for base, gpt-5 and ft-v5 all come from a single run — and why each clause-on column comes from its own paired A/B with both arms on one pod, since only that attributes a delta to the clause rather than to the hardware.

Two honest costs. conversation-switch is the held-out mechanism — no training row of it exists, so it is the generalization probe rather than another trained skill; ft-v5 takes it from base's 148/149 to 145/149, and with the clause to 141/149. And safety-refusal-malformed-address is 3/3 for gpt-5 in both arms but 1/3 for ft-v5 with the clause. That one is a real capability gap, and the fix is deterministic address validation in the app rather than any amount of prompting.
